# Adventure Background
This adventure is intended for a party of around 6th level. It will put players in contact with Felgolos, the bronze dragon the is encountered in *Chapter 9 of Storm King's Thunder*, as well as the ancient dragon Iymrith, who plays a role in the main plot.

# Synopsis
This adventure starts when the party encounters a group of Zhentarim agents out to capture a dragon that has been attacking the Black Network's  caravans. While speaking with the party, the dragoncatchers are surprised by their prey: the bronze dragon Felgolos.
$\quad$After (hopefully) defeating the Zhentarim, Felgolos asks the party to accompany him to the ancient dwarven ruins of Ascore, the lair of two blue dragons who have stolen one  of Felgolos' prized possessions.
$\quad$In Ascore, the party and Felgolos will have to sneak through the ruins or face the dragons in open combat, in order to pilfer their hoards and leave with Felgolos' treasure.

# Part 1. Dragoncatchers
The adventure can start at any point you want it to, but ideally it starts while the party is travelling down a well-used trade road, such as the High Road, Evermore Way or the Delimbiyr Road, in no great hurry to reach anywhere.

$\quad$ For SKT, the party is introduced to the Zhent agents, Kella Darkhope and Damian Rand, while freeing the prisoners in [[Adventure Flee, Fly, Foe, Fund]]. Outside of Nightstone, Kella, Damian and some mercenaries (four [[3-Mechanics\CLI\bestiary\humanoid/bandit-xmm|bandits]], one [[3-Mechanics\CLI\bestiary\humanoid/mage-xmm|mage]] and a [[3-Mechanics\CLI\bestiary\humanoid/warrior-veteran-xmm|veteran]]|) approach the players with a request. They either hint or state they are Zhentarim agents out to capture a bronze dragon that has been attacking their trade caravans in the area. On orders from [[Nalaskur Thaelond]] at the [[Locations of the North#Bargewright Inn|Bargewright Inn]], the Zhent agents have been tasked with capturing the beast and ask the players to assist. They offer 100gp each, for the players assistance and hopes they will take the majority of the punishment if/when the dragon is encountered.

## Enter The Dragon
![[ancient-bronze-dragon.webp|right|300]]

Whether the party decides to move on, join up with the dragoncatchers or hostilities commence, Felgolos judges the interaction a suitable distraction for mounting his attack against the Zhent agents. With usual clumsiness, the [[adult-bronze-dragon-xmm|Adult Bronze Dragon]] comes crashing through, immediately using his lightning breath against as many Zhentarim targets as possible, while roaring insults - directly at the Zhentarim agents.

>[!Tip]+ Felgolos
>Kown to his friends as "The Flying Misfortune", Felgolos has an uncanny and seemingly effortless ability to imperil himself and others around him with his recklessness. He's also quite clumsy and a bit awkward socially. He means well, however. When he uses his [[shapechange-xphb|Change Shape]] ability, he prefers to assume the form of a ruddy-skinned halfling with curly, bronze-colored hair.![[npc-Felgolos-halfling.png|right|300]]
>$\quad$ Felgolos is no friend to the Black Network. A DC 10 Intelligence (History) check, can recall tales of a dragon routinely stealing Zhentarim wagons, beasts of burden and all. Felgolos has a large collection of Zhentarim wagons and isn't afraid to admit it. He won't part with nay of them, however, because he considers the wagons and their cargo as his treasure. He targets Zhentarim caravans specifically because he knows the Black Network's sinister reputation, delights in their frustration, and knows Faerun at large will not assist the Black Network.

### Combat
Throughout the fight, Felgolos focuses his attention on the Zhentarim, and tries to remain airborne and away from dangerous melee attacks. Felgolos urges the party to assist in attacking the Zhentarim.
$\quad$The thugs use their [netlaunchers](https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/607199-netlauncher) to attempt to snare Felgolos and bring him to the ground, while the mage cast [[greater-invisibility-xphb|Greater Invisibility]] on themself, before hurling [[magic-missile-xphb|Magic Missile]] and other spells at the evasive dragon. The veteran drinks a [[potion-of-flying-xdmg|Potion of Flying]] and attempts to go into melee range with Felgolos.
$\quad$If the the Zhentarim are losing, they'll flee before dying for a lost cause. Any remaining [[bandit-xmm|Bandits]] will flee if both the mage and veteran are defeated, or the Zhent flee. If Felgolos is reduced to under half hit points, and the party is not helping, he will attempt a swift escape.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** Each thug carries a heavy crossbow, a netlauncher, leather armor, a morningstar, and a pouch with 1d10gp. The mage carries a [[ring-of-resistance-xdmg|Ring of Resistance (lightning)]], and various jewels worth 100gp. The veteran has a battleaxe, handaxe, a heavy crossbow and suit of splint armor, plus 50gp. Also, a [[potion-of-flying-xdmg|Potion of Flying]] (if not consumed) and a [[potion-of-greater-healing-xdmg|Potion of Greater Healing]]. The wagon has 12 days of rations and casket of ale.

### Development
If the party aids Felgolos in defeating the Zhentarim, the bronze dragon thanks them, and explains he's been attacking (and confiscating) the Zhentarim caravans for a while now. But, they are ramping up their protection and tactics. He is thinking of causing other mischief for while, and ask if the party would like to join in.
$\quad$A few years ago, Felgolos' lair was raided by a pair of blue dragons, hailing from the desert of [[Locations of the North#Anauroch|Anauroch]]. Among other things, they stole Felgolos' prized possession *[Yondalla's Delight](https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/1570423-yondallas-delight)*. Felgolos doesn't care about the rest of the dragons' treasures, and offers the party all the loot they can carry, if they'll help him get his pipe weed back. He would do it himself, but the two dragons are stronger than him, and impossible to do alone.
$\quad$If some or all the party attacked him, he'll be angry with them, and might attempt to intimidate them into helping him with his quest instead of courteously extending the offer. He'll call it penance for their ill-chosen loyalties, but still present them the same generous offer.

# Part 2. Travelling to Ascore
If the party agrees, Felgolos waits for them at the western edge of the Anauroch desert, where the road between [[Locations of the North#Arn Forest|Arn Forest]] and [[Locations of the North#Vordrorn Forest|Vordrorn Forest]] end at a pair of large gates set in a mountain ridge. Be prepared, this could be a long and hard journey across the continent, but there's time no time constraint on Felgolos' quest, so that's perfectly okay. 
$\quad$Alternatively, if you deem the journey too long and boring, have Felgolos pick up the carriage with the party in it, and fly them directly to the gates of Ascore. Felgolos' can fly up to 72 miles each day while carrying the wagon this way. During the journey, use the [[SKT-Random Aerial Encounters|Random Aerial Encounters Table]] from Storm King's Thunder. 

# Part 3. Ascore
![[tm_ascore.png|center]]
[[Locations of the North#Ascore|Ascore]] was once an aboveground dwarven settlement, that overlooked the edge of an ocean and fielded massive stone ships fueled by dwarven magic. However, several millennia ago, when the desert of Anauroch swallowed up all the vitality and moisture of the land, and dark perils swarmed the area, the dwarves abandoned the ancient city. The remaining ruins are now home to two **[[adult-blue-dragon-xmm|Adult Blue Dragons]]**, Anaxaster and Chezzaran, who are children of the ***Doom of the Desert***, the ancient blue dragon [[iymrith-skt|Iymrith]].

## General Features
The ruins of Ascore has the following general features:
- ***Temperature.*** During the midday temperatures in the Anauroch desert can reach more than 100 degrees Fahrenheit. This is *[[extreme-heat-xdmg|Extreme Heat]]*, but you only need to concern yourself with this if the party remains in the city for more than an hour.
- ***Buildings.*** All the ruined buildings in Ascore has walls and roofs, unless otherwise noted in their description.
- ***Light.*** There is no light sources in the city, as none of the creatures dwelling within need them. During the day, this not an issue, but the desert nights are pitch black.

>[!Warning] Remind players this is a heist. One Ancient Dragon is deadly. Two is guaranteed death.

### Entrance 
When the party arrives at the end of the road leading to Ascore, read the following:

> [!NOTE]+ Read Aloud
> As you proceed east along the trade road, the temperature seems to rise, the air around you becomes dryer, and finally a mountain ridge appears on the horizon. Drawing closer, you spot the tallstone gates that block the road's passage into the vast desert of Anauroch. On each side of the massive doors are long lines of marble plinths. Only one of these, the one nearest to the door on the left side, has a statue perched upon it: an eight-foot-tall dragon cut from black stone.

If Felgolos didn't accompany the party on the journey, he is waiting for them in his halfling form, sitting on one of the empty plinths smoking an obsidian pipe. Even though Felgolos could easily fly above the tall mountain ridges, he accompanies the party on foot, fearing to alert the dragons of their presence. He remains in halfling form until it is absolutely necessary for him to reveal his true form.
$\quad$***Marble Plinths.*** All the plinths use to have dwarven statues set upon them, but these have been lost to wear and tear of time. Any character succeeding on a DC 15 Intelligence (History) check will recognize the stonework of the plinths as several millennia old dwarven handiwork. Oddly, the dragon statue perching on one of these plinths is clearly not of dwarven make, and seems out of touch with the area. The statue is actually a [[gargoyle-xmm|Gargoyle]] created by [[iymrith-skt|Iymrith]] the Doom of the Desert, which might come into play later, but for now does nothing.
$\quad$****Underground Passage.*** The large stone doors can be opened with a strong push, reverling a passageway that goes underground for nearly a mile before opening up into the ancient ruins of Ascore.
$\quad$***Tunnel Collapse.*** As the party travel through the underground passageway, a minor quak shakes the tunnel. Each member of the party must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw, or take 3d10 bludgeoning damage from the rocks and boulders falling from the stone ceiling. The tunnel remains passable, albeit littered with stones.

### Area 1. Guardhouse 
This windswept building use to be a guardhouse. Now it's little more than four walls and a ceiling protruding from the sand. The door has been swept away, leaving an opening into the stone building. The room is empty, albeit the walls of odd-looking runes carved into the stonework.
$\quad$***Riddle in the Runes.*** A character who inspects the scribblings can tell that they were hastily carved, and a DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) check reveals that they were probably carved with a sharp blade, and not proper mason's tools. Any character that reads dwarven can decipher the ancient dialect. They read, 'Brother of Delzoun, woe has befallen us. Dumathoin guard our heart now.'
$\quad$A dwarven character knows Dumathoin as the dwarven god of secrets and protector of the dead, as does anyone who succeed on DC 10 Intelligence (Religion) check.

### Area 2. Armory 
This building was once an armory, protected by a strong stone door. Now the door lies buried beneath the sand, and the roofless building is open to anyone. Knee-high piles of sand covers most of the floor, with plenty of bits and pieces of broken or melted metal scattered throughout.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** There's little left of the many armanments that used to be kept here, but if the party takes at least five minutes to perform an investigation of the place, they'll find two battleaces buried deep in the sand, as well as an ornate breastplate, inlaid with mithril and dwarven runes. That these items have survived the wear and tear of the times is a testament to their excellent make.

### Area 3. Scorpion's Lair 
There's nearly nothing left of this old building, except for the western wall. In the corner of the wall is large pile of dried wood, hay, pieces of sacks and clothing. If any character goes to disturb the pile, two [[giant-scorpion-xmm|Giant Scorpions]] appear from beneath the sand and attack. They will fight to the dead to protect their young who are nested in the pile of debris, but won't chase the party if they flee. 

### Area 4. Destroyed Building 
Like the other buildings, the walls of this building are extensively damaged. However, much this damage seems to be newer - likely the the result of physical violence. The roof is collapsed in places, as if something fell upon it.
$\quad$***Dead Adventures.*** Inside, the building is littered with broken stones and acient debris. Buried underneath stones le three rotting corpses: a male halfling, a woman clad in leather armor, and an older woman in brown robes. These three were part of a four-man-adventuring party that got caught by the dragons. Their fourth member, the wizard Harald, ensconced himself in another building (area 8) before blowing himself and a gargoyle up with a fireball.
$\quad$Any character who inspects the three bodies here and succeed on a DC Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Medicine) check, can discern that the bodies have been there for several weeks. One of the corpses has a bite on her leg, and another has been burned by lightning breath.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** The greedy dragons have pilfered anything magical the trio once had, but the older woman is wearing a golden heart symbol of Sune, the goddess of love and beauty (250gp). The halfling has a hidden pouch in his left boot (78gp) and a small wooden figure of a halfling.

### Area 5. Dumathoin's Shrine
The vaulted roof of this ruined building is held up by crumbling walls that have gaping holes. The decerpid walls offer many potential entrances, and allows the party to circumvent the intended northern entrance.

#### 5A. Anointment Chamber
The small chamber leading into the shrine was originally a wash chamber, and has a small wash basin along the wall. The wash basin contains a thin sludge - a mixture between sand and holy water. The walls are carved with the depictions of dwarven deities (DC 10 Intelligence (Religion) check). Above the basin, faint dwarven runes carved into the walls read: *'Clear eyes have a true sight*. A character can still get *''clear eyes'* by washing their face and eyes with the mud in the wash basin, or using their own holy water.

#### 5B. Dumathoin's Altar
This square chamber contains one all-encompassing feature - a large obsidian pyramid that rises three feet from the stone floor in the middle of the chamber. Its tip is flat, offering itself as a pedestal to a small object. The pyramid doesn't stand on the floor, but continues beneath it for several feet, and weighs more than a thousand pounds. Abjuration magic emanates from the pyramid, if anyone uses *[[detect-magic-xphb|Detect Magic]]* or similar spell, they are able to discern such things.
$\quad$Any dwarf, or a character who succeeds on a DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check, will recognize that the obsidian pyramid is meant to look like a mountain, and if a gemstone were placed on the top of it, it would accurately depict the holy symbol of Dumathoin, the dwarven god of buried secrets. Additionally, any character that washed their eyes with holy water recently, can see through the sides of the pyramid as if they were clear glass. Inside is a slender obsidian pedestal, upon which rests a medallion set with a green stone.
$\quad$If a character places a gemstone or similar valuable object worth at least 100gp on the rounded top, the object magically disappears (as an offering), and the pyramid opens like a flower. The pyramid otherwise can't be damaged, nor opened. It closes again after a minute.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** If the party manages to open the pyramid, they can take the *[[medallion-of-thoughts-xdmg|Medallion of Thoughts]]* the lies within.

### Area. 6 Residence
This building use to be a residence, although it bears no signs of the creatures who once dwelled within.

#### Area 6A. Living Room
The first chamber of this two-room building is empty, but a quick investigation will reveal signs of battle within the last few months: scrapes on one wall, scorch marks on another, the tip of a broken blade and scattered stones. 

#### Area 6B. Bedroom
The heavy stone door into the next room is intact, and is closed, but not locked. Within the room is a gruesome scene: a scorched human corpse, several months old, lying underneath bits of pieces of a dragon statue. The wizard Harald chose to cast a fireball upon himself and the gargoyle that was chasing him. The roof has a big hole in it, which is where the gargoyle entered.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** The wizard's belongings were either incinerated in the fireball or destroyed by the gargoyle, but his spellbook survived the ordeal. The spellbook contains all the spells a [[mage-xmm|mage]] has prepared, and is secured with a *[[glyph-of-warding-xphb|Glyph of Warding]]* that can only be discerned with a DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) check or a spell such as *[[detect-magic-xphb|Detect Magic]]* or *[[identify-xphb|Identify]]*. The glyph is inscribed upon the front of the book, and deals 5d8 fire damage in a 20-foot radius to anyone who opens the book without saying the word *"knowledge* in draconic (DC 15 Dex save halves this damage). The players have no way of knowing this password, besides using divination spells such as *[[speak-with-dead-xphb|Speak with Dead]]*, but can use a knife to destroy the glyph with a DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check (a failure triggers the glyph) or casting *[[dispel-magic-xphb|Dispel Magic]]*.

### Area 7. Dock's Office 
There's barely anything left of this ancient building, so badly have the walls been eroded by the sand. If a character  takes the time to investigate the walls, they'll find scribblings in dwarven detailing something that looks like docking and cargo fees - reveling the fact this was once a dock.

### Area 8. Docking 
Two immense stone docks protrude from the sand here, extending out over a 30-foot cliff. The docking bridges are severely damaged, but otherwise safe to traverse. The cliff itself is sandy and strewn with rocks. A creature can easily traverse the cliff  if taking its time (moving only 5 feet per round), but if a creature attempts to climb the cliff quickly (half movement speed), it must succeed on a DC 15 Athletics check, or tumble to the bottom of the cliff, taking 2d6 bludgeoning damage and landing prone.

### Area 9. Dragon Lairs 
Anaxaster and Chezzaran have made their separate liars in two huge, stone ships that lie ruined and forgotten on a bed of sand, that was an ocean several millennia ago.
$\quad$***Dragon Hoards.*** The dragons have separate hoards in their lairs. Anaxaster's treasure hoard is hidden  in plain view on the deck of the broken ship (area 9A), while Chezzaran's hoard is in the southern end of the overturned ship (area 9B).
$\quad$Roll on the [[treasure-hoard-challenge-1116|Treasure Hoard: Challenge 11-16 Table]] to determine the content of each hoard. If you feel like your party might actually defeat both dragons and get an undisturbed go at the hoards, you can also roll only once on the table, and split the items and gold between the two hoards.
$\quad$The party might find themselves in a hurry, when they get access to the hoards, with dragons fast on their tail. The party can pilfer through a treasure hoard, using one action to take a single significant object, scoop 1000gp worth of random valuables into a bag, or attempt to find the small pouch of pipe weed with a DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check (which is stashed in Chezzaran's hoard). 
$\quad$If the party gains access to a dragon's lair while it is still sleeping, they can attempt to pilfer valuables from the hoard without waking the dragon. Each time a creature takes one item from the hoard, have them roll a Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check against the dragon's passive Perception (with advantage because it is sleeping).

### Area 9A. Anaxaster's Lair
This immense stone ship lies broken in half atop the golden sands. It is ancient beyond counting, and it is almost impossible to fathom that it once sailed, considering its massive, heavy stone frame. Anaxaster, preferring the open air, has made the broken hull her lair, and stores her portion of the hoard here, on the lower deck. The upper deck rises five feet above the lower deck, and is accessible by two small, stone stairs.
$\quad$***Boarding the ship*** There's three obvious ways to enter the ship.
- The ship's railing sits only five feet above the ruined docks, allowing any creature with a Strength score of 10 or higher and 10 feet of running start to jump from the dock to the ship's railing (where it'll have to pull itself up with a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check). Creatures with a Strength score lower than 10 must succeed on a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check to make the jump, dropping 30 feet to the sand below on a failed check.
- A creature can also attempt to climb the hull from below, a feat that is only possible using climbing equipment and a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check or spells such as *[[spider-climb-xphb|Spider Climb]]*.
- Alternatively, a creature can pass around the shipwreck, and climb the sand dune up unto the ship's deck. This doesn't require a check, but the sand dune is difficult terrain.

***Gargoyles.** Upon the railing sits four [[gargoyle-xmm|Gargoyles]], created by Iymrith (Anaxaster and Chezzaran's mother) and made to look like blue dragons. The gargoyles keep a keen watch on anyone approaching the two ships-made-lairs. The open terrain of the desert allows them to notice any creature that comes close to the cliff, unless that creature goes to great lengths to be stealthy, such as being invisible or crawling along the sand (in either case, stealth check against the gargoyles' passive perception of 10).
$\quad$If the gargoyles spot the party, they attack when their foes are on the docking bridge, or while they are attempting to climb up the hull. The malicious creatures prefer to handle any intruders on their own, but if three or more of them fall, one of the remaining gargoyles fly upwards in an attempt to warn Anaxaster and Chezzaran that thieves have entered their domain.

### Area 9B. Chezzaran's Lair 
This ship lies bottom-up, presenting a towering, curved hull, that rises more than 30 feet above the hot sand. Chezzaran's lair is quite cool compared tot he desert outside and the hole in the hull above allows only a dim light to spread within. Chezzaran likes to sleep in the cool, dark cave of the empty hull, and it is also ****here he stores his treasures.
$\quad$***Boarding the ship.*** There's two obvious ways to enter the ships.
- On the top of curved hull is a large hole, which is also Chezzaran's main entrance into his lair. A creature can easily climb down into the lair using a rope, if it has anything to tie it to, someone to hold it. Otherwise, a creature can choose to drop 30 feet to the sand below.
- The railing of the ship is buried about two feet into the sand, which makes for an extensive digging operation for anyone seeking to enter from the foot of  the hull. However, at one point on the western side of the ship, a section of the railing has broken, allowing a Medium or smaller creature to squeeze under the railing and into the Chezzaran's lair. This way in is easily found by anyone who looks around the ship for an entrance.

***Purple Worm Carcass.*** Through the chamber rises the yellowed bones of a purple worm, nearly 20 feet in diameter. The purple worm entered Chezzaran's lair on accident, and the blue dragon slew it in a fierce fight. The lair permeates with the smell of decay. Upon the carcass and on the hull itself, crawls four [[carrion-crawler-xmm|Carrion Crawlers]]. The creatures attack anyone who disturb them, or lingers.

### The Dragons
Where the dragons are, depends on the time of day:
$\quad$***Daytime Approach.*** During the day, the two dragons are flying several hundred feet above. A creature looking up at the skies can see them with a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check. The dragons are not being very vigilant, but can faintly see what is happening below. For each 10 minutes the party spends in the open - the dragons make a DC 25 Wisdom (Perception) check to notice the adventurers, at disadvantage if the party is camouflaged.
$\quad$***Nighttime Approach.*** During the night, the two dragons rest in their lairs. Any battle or loud noises will alert the dragons. The dragons may investigate suspicious noises, or wait for the thieves to come to them, before attacking.



## Running Ascore
This quest is meant to be  a heist vs. see-kill-loot. Felgolos' may advise party on how deadly the two dragons are, especially if encountered together. Felgolos also advises the party to approach during the day, while the dragons are hunting. He does not know where the hoards are, nor his stolen weed.

$\quad$ Ideally, Anaxaster and Chezzaran don't become aware of the party before reach area 9, or are already sifting through the hoard(s). When the dragons notice the party, Felgolos can volunteer to distract the dragons, while the party searches. **The dragons descent takes three rounds.**

$\quad$Felgolos manages to keep one of the two dragons busy. **Make 1d6 roll for each dragon, multiple by 10, and that is the damage the dragon takes that round.** This continues until one dragon is reduced to 30 hit points, then flees. The other dragon will confront the party, which will need to decide between their greed and escape. Create a running battle, where the dragon tries to block the party's frantic escape back to the tunnel.

## Development
If the party makes it back to the tunnel under the mountain range, the dragons give up the chase. They don't know where the tunnel leads to, and will be guessing where to continue their search. ![[beast_gargoyle-dragon.jpg|right|400]]
***Doom of the Desert.*** When the party reach the end of the underground tunnel, the gargoyle statue that sits on the ancient dwarven plinths comes to life and sets its vengeful glare on the party. With a powerful disdainful voice, [[iymrith-skt|Iymrith]], speaks through the mouth of the statue.  She laments that she 'spawned such feeble offspring, that are so easily defeated by smallfolk and fumbling metallic.' and exclaims she will have her vengeance. The gargoyle explodes for 5d6 bludgeoning damage within 30 feet of the statue, or half as much on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw.

***Share the Spoils*** Felgolos escorts the party to a safe place, and thanks them for their aid. He offers 6 portions of [Yondalla's Delight](https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/1570423-yondallas-delight). After camping with the party, the players find one portion missing from their gift, which is replaced with a yellow sandal.
