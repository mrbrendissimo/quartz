#### 3. Mill and Water Wheel

- Mill
	- An enormous wooden water wheel is attached to an iron axle and mounted above a swift-flowing river that spills down the mountainside. 
		- As the current turns the wheel, it not only powers the elevator, but also turns a large millstone located inside a hut 
		- The grinding of the millstone, the cranking of the water wheel, and the rush of water pouring down the mountainside drown out most other noises in this area.

- Water Wheel
	- Characters can try to reach the elevator by climbing through the openings in the frame of the water wheel and crawling along its iron axle. 
		- This is a dangerous act, DC 15 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check.
		- A character who fails the check doesn't make it across and must make a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw. 
			- On a failed save, the character is swept down the river and tumbles down the mountainside, taking 70 (20d6) bludgeoning 
			- On a successful save, the character manages to hang onto the wheel or the axle and avoid a fall, but is stuck between the spokes of the wheel and must repeat the check

- Breaking the Water Wheel
	- Jamming the water wheel disables it, when the wheel is stopped, the force of the current puts tremendous strain on it. 
		- At the end of each turn, the wheel has a 25 percent chance of breaking apart and tumbling down the waterfall. 
		- If that happens, the millstone and the elevator cease to function. Any creature on the wheel swept over the cliff and fire giants investigate

## Minecart

**Rule 1**: Rounds begin by picking positions: front, back, left, and right. Positions decide what roles you can play that round, ie: steering, cranking, and fighting boarders.

**Rule 2**: The minecart will naturally slow down. To maintain speed with four passengers, one person must crank. To speed up, two people must crank.

**Rule 3**: When slowed, the horde can make ranged attacks. When at speed, the attacks take disadvantage. When sped up, you are out of their range for that round. This resets at the beginning of the each round, though overall speed is kept into account.

**Rule 4**: Cranking takes an action. When cranking, your bonus action can be to do a strength check for a short speed boost to impose penalties on boarders, steer the cart to different tracks (front position), or melee boarders behind you (back position, strength contest with penalty). The player in the back position can choose to attach instead of crank. Talking is a free action.

**Rule 5**: If you are on the side positions, you can only attack boarders on your side and adjacent areas (front, back).

**Rule 6**: Players and boarders alternate turns every round, choosing who will use their actions at will. The horde will always attack last.

**Rule 7**: Different tracks chosen have different advantages, disadvantages, and ability checks. The route taken is ultimately the front player's decision.

**Rule 8**: If a player is knocked unconscious, they can be moved, but it takes the moving player's bonus action.

"Boarders" will be able to attack players directly, try to ram the cart to destabilize the crankers (strength vs constitution contest), and grapple players or the cart to slow it down. Some of the track obstacles include raining stalactites (damage), inclines over chasms (strength check to slow boarders), downward slopes through an underground waterfall (speed boost for a constitution check), and tracks that go by a wall with a jewel embedded in it (dexterity check for gold).

At the end of the track is a big gap. If they've been consistently speeding up, they clear it. If not, the crankers have a last-ditch strength check. If they fall that, the cart comes up short and they have to make a dexterity saving throw to jump out. If they fail that, they hit the wall on the other side and have to climb up while the horde takes pot-shots at them.

Exactly when they arrive is up to you, but here are some suggestions:

- If the characters slay [Duke Zalto](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#duke%20zalto_skt), the drow might arrive shortly thereafter and attack.
- If the characters are having an easy time in Ironslag, the drow might be with Zalto when the party finally confronts him. Draac doesn't give Zalto the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg) until the characters are defeated or forced to retreat.
- If the characters leave Ironslag without confronting [Duke Zalto](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#duke%20zalto_skt), the drow arrive after the party leaves and give Zalto the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg).

On their first turns in combat, both Draac and Taal attempt to summon a [shadow demon](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#shadow%20demon_mm). If defeat seems likely, Draac orders the remainder of his force to cover his escape while he flees with the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg).

### Treasure p186

In addition to the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg), Draac carries a nonmagical staff topped with web patterns on the haft and a sculpted obsidian spider with small diamonds for eyes at the top (worth 1,500 gp). Taal carries a pouch with four 100 gp gems inside it, and he wears a fine black cloak embroidered with webs made of platinum thread (worth 750 gp). The elite drow warriors carry no treasure.

## Zalto's Iron Flask p186

Tied to Draac's belt is an [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg) that contains [Maegera the Dawn Titan](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#maegera%20the%20dawn%20titan_skt) (see [appendix C](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#skt,15)), a mighty fire primordial that the drow stole from Gauntlgrym. Removing the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg)'s stopper frees Maegera from captivity, whereupon the primordial is compelled to obey the commands of whoever freed it for 1 hour. Once that hour has elapsed, the primordial goes berserk and attacks nearby creatures indiscriminately while incinerating its surroundings.

[Duke Zalto](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#duke%20zalto_skt) and the drow know that Maegera can't be controlled for long, so they won't release the fire primordial anywhere but inside Ironslag's adamantine forge ([area 29](https://5e.tools/adventure.html#skt,8,29.%20adamantine%20forge,0)). If Draac is unable to give the flask to Zalto and is forced to flee, he keeps the flask with him and returns to the Underdark with it, determined to keep it out of the hands of the dwarves of Gauntlgrym.

### Development p186

The characters might obtain the [iron flask](https://5e.tools/items.html#iron%20flask_dmg) and try to trap Maegera inside the magic furnace aboard their airship (see the "Airship of a Cult" section in chapter 4). The airship's furnace can hold Maegera, but only briefly. After each hour of confinement, Maegera can make a DC 15 Strength saving throw to escape confinement. If it fails the saving throw, Maegera remains trapped for another hour. If the saving throw succeeds, Maegera breaks free and goes berserk, destroying the furnace in the process.

#### Character Advancement p186

After obtaining [Duke Zalto](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#duke%20zalto_skt)'s [conch of teleportation](https://5e.tools/items.html#conch%20of%20teleportation_skt), the characters might want to hole up somewhere so that one of them can attune to the conch and use it to teleport the party to Maelstrom. The characters might also delay using the conch because they want to continue exploring Ironslag or because they have some loose ends to tie up, such as coming to terms with [Jasper Dimmerchasm](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#jasper%20dimmerchasm_skt), helping [Cinderhild](https://5e.tools/bestiary.html#cinderhild_skt) escape the clutches of her parents, or returning Maegera to Gauntlgrym. In any event, allow the characters to advance to 9th level before moving on to chapter 10, "Hold of the Storm Giants."
