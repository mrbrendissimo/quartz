# The Long Dark: A Fire Below

A chase-based dungeon crawl for 4 adventurers (built for the 2024 D&D rules / "5.5e," levels 5–8; scale monster stats up/down as needed).

---

## The Premise

The party has triggered (or stumbled into) the release of **Cinderjaw**, a bound fire elemental that has haunted this mine since a dwarven expedition tried to weaponize a volcanic vein a century ago. It cannot be killed by conventional means — it simply reforms a few rounds later wherever fire still burns. The only way out is *through* the mine, to the old ore-cart rail line that leads to the surface.

**The twist you asked for:** the mine itself is full of half-finished dwarven contraptions, flooded side-shafts, and forge rooms — tools the party can turn into weapons against something made of fire.

---

## Core Mechanic: The Pursuit Track

Don't track exact squares — track **abstract distance** so the tension stays high without slowing down combat math.

- Create a **Pursuit Track** with 8 segments. Cinderjaw starts at segment 0 (far behind); the cart landing is segment 8.
- Each round, describe a "beat" — the party in the current room, Cinderjaw's roars/light/heat getting closer.
- **At the end of each round**, roll or assign Cinderjaw's advance:
  - Normally it advances **1 segment**.
  - If the party **actively hinders it** (below), it advances **0** that round, or even loses a segment.
  - If the party **wastes an action economy turn** (fails a check badly, argues, backtracks), it advances **2**.
- When Cinderjaw reaches the party's segment, it gets one turn to act before the party moves to the next room (see "When It Catches Up").
- The party reaches the carts at segment 8. If Cinderjaw is within 1 segment when they do, the escape sequence gets one final Group Save (below).

This means every room is a decision: *push forward fast, or spend a round rigging something that buys distance.*

---

## When It Catches Up

Cinderjaw is unkillable, but not undamageable — damage just **staggers** it (loses its next action) rather than reducing it to 0 HP meaningfully. Give it:

- **Cinderjaw, Bound Flame** — treat as the **Fire Elemental** from the 2024 Monster Manual, with these changes:
  - Immune to fire, resistant to physical damage from non-magical/non-cold sources.
  - **Vulnerable to cold damage** (unchanged from the base stat block — this matters, see the ice room).
  - **Regeneration:** heals to full 1 minute after being reduced to 0 HP, reforming from any nearby flame or ember within 30 ft. It does *not* come back if there is no fire source nearby — which is why dousing rooms matter.
  - On catching the party: it doesn't get a "kill" round automatically. It attacks once (fire damage + chance to ignite flammable gear), and unless the party clears 1 full segment of distance next round, it stays adjacent.
  - **Weapon Mastery tie-in (2024 rules):** if your players have martial weapons with mastery properties, this fight is a great place to let them shine even though Cinderjaw can't be "killed" — **Topple** (Str save or knocked prone, costing it its next move) or **Slow** (reduces its speed, buying the party ground) both work great as flavor for "staggering" it without needing new mechanics.

This keeps fights from becoming attrition slogs — the goal is always "get away," not "grind it down."

## The Rooms (Segments 1–7)

Each room is a puzzle/hazard *and* a lever against Cinderjaw. Use 5–6 of these depending on how long you want the chase; drop or reorder freely.

### 1. The Flooded Cistern
An old water reservoir, waist-deep, with a rusted sluice gate.
- **Puzzle:** The gate wheel is jammed; a DC 14 Str check or a clever use of a lever/tool frees it.
- **Turn it against the monster:** Opening the sluice floods the corridor behind them. If Cinderjaw is adjacent or one segment back when it opens, it's doused — **stagger it for a full round** and it doesn't regenerate until it reaches a new flame source. Strong incentive to time this right.

### 2. The Bellows Hall
A chamber of massive dwarven forge-bellows, still rigged to leather tubing that vents to the tunnels ahead.
- **Puzzle:** Pumping the bellows (2 characters working together, 1 round) redirects a blast of air.
- **Turn it against the monster:** A blast of air won't hurt fire — but reversed, the bellows can *starve* a fire elemental's flame by sucking oxygen from a sealed side-tunnel. If the party lures Cinderjaw into the sealed alcove first (a Deception/Performance check to bait it, or simply positioning), sealing the vent and reversing the bellows deals ongoing damage until it breaks free (DC 16 Str/Con to escape the vacuum pocket).

### 3. The Gear Bridge
A collapsed section of walkway, spanned only by an enormous rotating gear mechanism still partially functional.
- **Puzzle:** Timing jumps between rotating gear teeth (DC 12 Dex, or auto-success if someone stabilizes it from the control lever first).
- **Turn it against the monster:** The gear can be jammed mid-rotation (DC 15 Str, or wedging a weapon into it — permanently breaks that weapon). A jammed gear collapses the walkway entirely one round later. If Cinderjaw is mid-crossing, it falls into the shaft below — costs it 2 segments of pursuit ground while it climbs back out.

### 4. The Ice Vein
A rare frozen mineral seam, unnaturally cold, likely magical in origin (dwarves mined near it for cooling their forges).
- **Puzzle:** Chipping free a chunk of the vein ice (DC 13 Investigation to find a loose section, or just brute-force it) gives the party 1–2 uses of **improvised cold weapons/throwables** for the rest of the session — treat as dealing an extra 1d6 cold damage, which (per Cinderjaw's vulnerability) counts as full damage against its regeneration, not just staggering it.
- This is the room to make sure they don't miss — cold is the one resource that does *lasting* damage to Cinderjaw instead of just buying time.

### 5. The Rail Switch
An intersection of ore-cart rails with a manual switch lever, mostly rusted in place.
- **Puzzle:** Throwing the switch (DC 12 Str, easier with a tool as a lever) redirects an empty ore cart already rolling down a side track.
- **Turn it against the monster:** Timed right, the redirected cart slams into Cinderjaw, staggering it and knocking it back a segment. Timed wrong, it just clatters through empty — a good "was that worth the round we spent" moment.

### 6. The Unstable Support Chamber
A room shored up with rotting timber, clearly ready to come down.
- **Puzzle:** No real check needed — this is a "spend a round, get a payoff" room. Chopping/collapsing a support beam (any character with a weapon, 1 action) with a DC 13 Dex save for anyone still inside when it drops.
- **Turn it against the monster:** A full collapse blocks the corridor behind the party — Cinderjaw must either dig through (costing it 1 full round, i.e., it doesn't advance) or find an alternate route (you decide, or roll a d4 — on a 1, it finds a shortcut and skips ahead a segment instead, keeping tension alive).

### 7. The Chasm Crossing
A wide crack in the mine floor, magma glow visible far below, spanned by a narrow, half-broken cart-rail bridge.
- **Puzzle:** Crossing safely takes a DC 14 Dex/Acrobatics check per character, or someone can stabilize the bridge from an anchor winch (DC 15 Str) to make it automatic for the rest of the party.
- **Danger, not lever:** This one isn't a trap-turned-weapon — it's pure tension. Cinderjaw doesn't care about the chasm (it can practically walk on the magma). This is the room to remind them the monster has zero respect for the hazards they're using — it will cross straight through, so this segment should cost *them* time, not it.

## The Forge Room (Optional Detour — the +2 Item)

Place this as a **side-branch off segment 4 or 5**, not on the direct path — reaching it costs the party a detour (advance Cinderjaw 1 extra segment to enter), which is the price for the reward.

**The Room:** An intact dwarven forge, untouched because it was sealed off after Cinderjaw's original binding failed. Everything needed to finish a masterwork item is here — an anvil still faintly warm, quenching oil, dwarven runic stamps.

- **The catch:** Finishing an item here takes **3 full rounds of uninterrupted work** by whoever's crafting (they can't also be fighting or fleeing that same round).
- **The rest of the party's job:** hold the door. Use any of the following to buy time without needing another full puzzle:
  - Barricading the forge door (DC 13 Str, buys 1 round).
  - Sacrificing one of the earlier hazards in reverse — e.g., they can backtrack to jam the Gear Bridge or reroute the Rail Switch cart *toward* the forge entrance instead of away, buying an extra round each.
  - A straight fight: Cinderjaw can be staggered repeatedly (any hit that drops its HP to 0 staggers it for a round) — treat this as a delay tactic, not a real fight.
- **If they succeed:** the crafter finishes a **+2 weapon, +2 armor, or comparable magic item of their choice**, freshly quenched and still faintly warm. This is a fiction-first reward for pulling off the delay under pressure, so it's fine to waive the gold/rare-material costs the 2024 DMG's crafting rules normally require — treat the forge itself and the danger of being here as the "cost." If you'd rather keep it tied to the formal system, you can instead let this scene satisfy the *time* requirement only, and still require the gold cost be paid later once they're back in town.
- **If they fail (Cinderjaw breaks in before 3 rounds are up):** the crafting is ruined, but let them keep whatever partial benefit feels right (e.g., a +1 version, or the item exists but is unattuned until repaired later) — don't make it feel like the detour was pointless.

## The Finale: The Cart Chase

At segment 8, the party reaches three battered ore carts sitting at the top of a long, steep rail line sloping down toward the mine mouth and daylight. This isn't a single skill challenge anymore — it's its own mini-chase, because **Cinderjaw has sent fire minions ahead** to cut the carts off before the party even gets moving.

**The Minions:** Use **Magma Mephits** (or reflavor as "Cinder Wisps" — small, jabbering fragments of Cinderjaw's flame, CR 1/2 each). Spawn 3–4 of them, one per party member, so everyone has something to deal with. They can cling to cart exteriors, run along the rails, or leap between carts — treat them as having a climb speed equal to their walk speed for this scene. They're easy to destroy individually (they don't regenerate like Cinderjaw does) but there are enough of them to be a real nuisance while everyone's also trying to steer.

**Structure:** Run this as **4 rounds** of simultaneous cart-riding + combat. Each round has two things happening — a **track hazard** (below) and **minion harassment** — and the party should be splitting actions between them (steering/jumping vs. fighting).

### Round 1 — Loose Rails
The carts lurch onto a stretch of track with missing spikes. **DC 13 Dex save or the cart driver loses control for a moment**, veering toward the tunnel wall (minor damage, no real danger yet — this is the "orientation" round).
- *Minions:* 2 Cinder Wisps leap onto the back of the rearmost cart. Whoever's riding it can fight them off (AC 11, a couple hits each drops one) or shove them off the side (Str check, contested).

### Round 2 — The Split
The rail forks. One path is a straight shot but runs directly under a weakened section of ceiling (spotted with a passive Perception 13, otherwise a nasty surprise); the other curves wide and costs time but is safe.
- **Choice, not a check:** taking the risky path saves a round of distance from Cinderjaw's main body catching up later, but anyone who doesn't call it in advance eats a **DC 14 Dex save or falling rubble damage** as they pass under.
- *Minions:* the remaining/new Wisps try to **uncouple a cart** from the one ahead of it (they're fiddling with the coupling pin) — someone needs to notice and stop them (Perception then a quick grab, or just spend an action swatting the mephit off) or that cart falls behind, stranding whoever's in it.

### Round 3 — The Firebreak
The tunnel here was deliberately burned out by whatever's left of the old mining operation — a section of collapsed, smoldering track with a gap in the rail. The carts genuinely can't cross this on rails.
- **Puzzle-in-motion:** someone has to improvise — jamming a weapon or tool into the rail junction to bridge the gap (DC 15, using an object you're willing to lose or damage), or unhooking and having everyone jump the gap manually while the cart free-wheels across on momentum alone (DC 13 Dex, failure = thrown from the cart, needs a hand up from an ally next round).
- *Minions:* this is their best shot — while everyone's focused on the gap, 1–2 fresh Wisps try to **ignite the cart's cargo/oil lines**, which if unchecked adds fire damage each subsequent round until put out (an action, no check needed, just costs someone a turn).

### Round 4 — Daylight
The tunnel widens, sloping hard toward the exit — Cinderjaw itself (not just minions) may make one last appearance here if it closed the gap earlier in the session, given a last dramatic lunge at the final cart before the tracks break into open air.
- **Group Skill Challenge (as before):** each player picks one last action — push the cart, cut the brake line, hold a minion back, brace an ally — and rolls a relevant check. 3 of 4 successes = a clean, cinematic escape into daylight; fewer successes = they make it out but eat a last parting blast of fire damage, or lose a piece of cargo/gear to the flames. Never a party death here — just cost.
- Cinderjaw itself does not follow onto the surface — bound to the mine, it roars and recedes into the dark. Any surviving Cinder Wisps simply gutter out in the sunlight, a nice visual beat to end on.

**Running the pacing:** don't call for individual initiative unless a fight actually breaks out — keep it narrative round-by-round (hazard, then "what's everyone doing about the minions"), and only drop into full turn order if someone's about to take a big hit or a minion's about to succeed at something bad. This keeps the cart chase feeling fast rather than turning into a stationary fight that happens to be on a moving cart.

## Running It At the Table

- Keep pace brisk: describe Cinderjaw's proximity every room (heat, light, the sound of rock melting) so the pursuit track stays felt, not just tracked on paper.
- Reward creativity generously — if a player proposes a way to use a room's hazard against Cinderjaw that isn't listed here, say yes and improvise a segment cost/benefit on the fly. The rooms above are a toolkit, not a script.
- The Ice Vein (room 4) is your safety valve — make sure the party finds it, since it's the only source of lasting damage rather than pure delay.
- For the cart chase, resist the urge to make every round a full combat encounter — the Cinder Wisps are there to create friction and split attention, not to be a real threat. If a round is going long, just narrate a Wisp getting swatted off and move to the next hazard.
