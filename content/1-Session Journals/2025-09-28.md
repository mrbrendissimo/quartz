# [[Adventure The Flying Misfortune#Part 3. Ascore|Part 3. Ascore]]

>[!Note]+ Read Aloud
>***Felgolos tells the party:*** [[Locations of the North#Ascore|Ascore]] was once an aboveground dwarven settlement, that overlooked the edge of an ocean and fielded massive stone ships fueled by dwarven magic. However, several millennia ago, when the desert of Anauroch swallowed up all the vitality and moisture of the land, and dark perils swarmed the area, the dwarves abandoned the ancient city. The remaining ruins are now home to two **[[adult-blue-dragon-xmm|Adult Blue Dragons]]**, Anaxaster and Chezzaran, who are children of the ***Doom of the Desert***, the ancient blue dragon [[iymrith-skt|Iymrith]].

## General Features
The ruins of Ascore has the following general features:
- ***Temperature.*** During the day temperatures is a 100 degrees Fahrenheit. This is *[[extreme-heat-xdmg|Extreme Heat]]* - an hour outside = bad stuff
- ***Light.*** There is no light sources in the city

>[!Warning] Remind players this is a heist. One Ancient Dragon is deadly. Two is guaranteed death. Felgolos also advises the party to approach during the day


### Entrance 
When the party arrives at the end of the road leading to Ascore, read the following:
> [!NOTE]+ Read Aloud
> As you proceed east along the trade road, the temperature seems to rise, the air around you becomes dryer, and finally a mountain ridge appears on the horizon. Drawing closer, you spot the tallstone gates that block the road's passage into the vast desert of Anauroch. On each side of the massive doors are long lines of marble plinths. Only one of these, the one nearest to the door on the left side, has a statue perched upon it: an eight-foot-tall dragon cut from black stone.

$\quad$***Marble Plinths.*** Any character succeeding on a DC 15 Intelligence (History) check will recognize the stonework of the plinths as several millennia old dwarven handiwork. 
*The dragon statue was created by [[iymrith-skt|Iymrith]]* 
$\quad$****Underground Passage.*** Large stone doors can be opened with a strong push, it is a mile long passageway into the ancient ruins of Ascore.
$\quad$***Tunnel Collapse.*** A minor quak shakes the tunnel. Each member of the party must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw, or take 3d10 bludgeoning damage from the rocks.

### Area 1. Guardhouse 
This windswept building use to be a guardhouse. Now it's little more than four walls and a ceiling protruding from the sand. The door has been swept away, leaving an opening into the stone building. The room is empty, albeit the walls of odd-looking runes carved into the stonework.
$\quad$***Riddle in the Runes.*** A DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) check reveals that they were not carved with mason's tools. In dwarven, the runes  read, 'Brother of Delzoun, woe has befallen us. Dumathoin guard our heart now.'
$\quad$ DC 10 Intelligence (Religion) check. - Dumathoin is the dwarven god of secrets and protector of the dead

### Area 2. Armory 
This building was once an armory, protected by a strong stone door. Now the door lies buried beneath the sand, and the roofless building is open to anyone. Knee-high piles of sand covers most of the floor, with plenty of bits and pieces of broken or melted metal scattered throughout.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** If party takes at least five minutes to perform an investigation of the place, they'll find two battle maces buried deep in the sand, as well as an ornate breastplate, inlaid with mithril and dwarven runes.

### Area 3. Scorpion's Lair 
There's nearly nothing left of this old building, except for the western wall. In the corner of the wall is large pile of dried wood, hay, pieces of sacks and clothing. 
*Two [[giant-scorpion-xmm|Giant Scorpions]] appear from beneath the sand and attack if disturbed.*

### Area 4. Destroyed Building 
Like the other buildings, the walls of this building are extensively damaged.

*[[passive-perception-xphb|Passive Perception]] Check* The damage is newer - likely the the result of physical violence. The roof is collapsed in places, as if something fell upon it.

$\quad$***Dead Adventures.*** Buried underneath stones - (3) rotting corpses: a male halfling, a woman clad in leather armor, and an older woman in brown robes. 

>[!tip]+ *These three were part of a four-man-adventuring party that got caught by the dragons. Their fourth member, the wizard Harald, ensconced himself in another building (area 8) before blowing himself and a gargoyle up with a fireball.*

$\quad$DC Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Medicine) check, bodies are 7 weeks old. One of the corpses has a bite on her leg, and another has been burned by lightning breath.
$\quad$***Treasure.*** corpse rob a golden heart symbol of Sune, the goddess of love and beauty (250gp). The halfling has a hidden pouch in his left boot (78gp) and a small wooden figure of a halfling.

### Area 5. Dumathoin's Shrine
The vaulted roof of this ruined building is held up by crumbling walls that have gaping holes. 

#### 5A. Anointment Chamber
Small chamber with a wash basin in the corner. If inspected, basin is mud inside. Faint carvings of more runes, and maybe dwarf figures.

DC 10 Intelligence (Religion) check) - Carvings on the wall depict dwarven deities Above the basin, faint dwarven runes carved into the walls read: *'Clear eyes have a true sight*. 

>[!tip]+ GM Note 
>A character can still get ''clear eyes' by washing their face and eyes with the mud in the wash basin, or using their own holy water.*

#### 5B. Dumathoin's Altar
This square chamber contains one all-encompassing feature - a large obsidian pyramid with a flat top rises (3)ft  from the stone floor in the middle of the chamber.

*Abjuration magic emanates from the pyramid, if anyone uses [[detect-magic-xphb|Detect Magic]] or similar spell, they are able to discern such things.*

$\quad$ DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check, the obsidian pyramid is looks like a mountain, and if a gemstone were placed on the top of it, it would accurately depict the holy symbol of Dumathoin, the dwarven god of buried secrets. 

>[!tip]+ GM Note
>Any character that washed their eyes with holy water recently, can see through the sides of the pyramid as if they were clear glass. Inside is a slender obsidian pedestal, upon which rests a medallion set with a green stone.
>*$\quad$If a character places a gemstone or similar valuable object worth at least 100gp on the rounded top, the object magically disappears (as an offering), and the pyramid opens like a flower. The pyramid otherwise can't be damaged, nor opened. It closes again after a minute.*
*$\quad$**Treasure.** If the party manages to open the pyramid, they can take the [[medallion-of-thoughts-xdmg|Medallion of Thoughts]] the lies within.*

### Area. 6 Residence
This building use to be a residence, although it bears no signs of the creatures who once dwelled within.

#### Area 6A. Living Room
Battered two-room building with signs of battle within the last few months: scrapes on one wall, scorch marks on another, the tip of a broken blade and scattered stones. 

#### Area 6B. Bedroom
A scorched human corpse, several months old, lying underneath bits of pieces of a dragon statue. There is a hole in the roof, and the interior of the room is scorched in a radius.

The wizard Harald chose to cast a fireball upon himself and the gargoyle that was chasing him. The roof has a big hole in it, which is where the gargoyle entered.

$\quad$***Treasure.*** 
- A spellbook secured with a *[[glyph-of-warding-xphb|Glyph of Warding]]*
- DC 18 Intelligence (Investigation) or Arcana check revels ward
- Deals 5d8 fire damage in a 20-foot radius to anyone who opens the book without saying the word *"knowledge* in draconic (DC 15 Dex save halves this damage). 
- The players have no way of knowing this password, besides using divination spells such as *[[speak-with-dead-xphb|Speak with Dead]]*
-  Can use a knife to destroy the glyph with a DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check (a failure triggers the glyph) or casting *[[dispel-magic-xphb|Dispel Magic]]*.

### Area 7. Dock's Office 
There's barely anything left of this ancient building, so badly have the walls been eroded by the sand. Scribblings in dwarven detailing docking and cargo fees - this was once a dock. 

### Area 8. Docking 
Two immense stone docks protrude from the sand here, extending out over a 30-foot cliff. The docking bridges are severely damaged (*but safe*) 

>[!tip]+ GM Read 
>The cliff itself is sandy and strewn with rocks. 
>A creature can easily traverse the cliff  if taking its time (moving only 5 feet per round), or DC 15 Athletics or Acrobatics check
>2d6 bludgeoning damage and prone

### The Dragons
Where the dragons are, depends on the time of day:
$\quad$***Daytime Approach.*** 
- During the day, the two dragons are flying several hundred feet above. 
- A creature looking up at the skies can see them with a DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) check. 
- For each 10 minutes the party spends in the open - the dragons make a DC 25 Wisdom (Perception) check
- The dragons don't become aware of the party before reach area 9, or start looting horde
- The dragons descent takes three rounds.

- $\quad$***Nighttime Approach.*** 
- During the night, the two dragons rest in their lairs. 
- Any battle or loud noises will alert the dragons. 
- The dragons may investigate suspicious noises, or wait for the thieves to come to them, before attacking.
